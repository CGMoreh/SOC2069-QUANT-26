Week 3 Self-check Quiz: Modelling continuous outcomes

Ten short questions to check your understanding after the Week 3 lecture. It takes about 10 minutes. Try each question before opening the answer. Most examples use the Spirit Level data (pickett2009) from the lecture and Worksheet 3: trust is the percentage of people who say most people can be trusted; income inequality is the ratio of the income of the richest fifth to that of the poorest fifth.

1. The slope

The regression of trust on income inequality gives: predicted trust = 77.06 − 6.54 × inequality. What does −6.54 mean?

  1. Trust falls by 6.54% in every country
  2. Countries whose inequality ratio is one unit higher have, on average, 6.54 percentage points lower trust
  3. Inequality explains 6.54% of trust
  4. The correlation between inequality and trust is −6.54
TipAnswer

b. The slope is the average difference in the outcome per one-unit difference in the predictor, in the outcome’s own units.

2. The intercept

What does the intercept, 77.06, describe here?

  1. The average trust across the 23 countries
  2. The predicted trust in a country with an inequality ratio of 0, which is impossible, so we don’t interpret it
  3. The maximum possible trust
  4. The trust of the most equal country
TipAnswer

b. The ratio can’t be below 1, so 0 lies far outside the data. The intercept is needed to draw the line but has no substantive meaning here. (The average trust, 38.8%, is the intercept of the null model M₀.)

3. Prediction

Using predicted trust = 77.06 − 6.535 × inequality, what is the predicted trust for Portugal (inequality = 8)?

  1. 10.0%
  2. 24.8%
  3. 38.8%
  4. 52.3%
TipAnswer

b. 77.06 − 6.535 × 8 = 24.8%. Portugal’s observed trust is 10.0%, so its residual is 10.0 − 24.8 = −14.8 points.

4. Residuals

What is a residual?

  1. The part of the outcome explained by the predictor
  2. The observed value minus the value predicted by the line
  3. The slope of the line
  4. A country with missing data
TipAnswer

b. Residuals are what the line gets wrong for each case. Least squares chooses the line that makes the sum of squared residuals as small as possible.

5. R²

The model has R² = 0.44. What does this mean?

  1. The slope is 0.44
  2. Inequality accounts for 44% of the variation in trust across these countries
  3. 44% of countries are correctly predicted
  4. Inequality causes 44% of a country’s trust
TipAnswer

b. R² is the share of the variation in the outcome that the model accounts for. The other 56% goes with other things. With one predictor, R² = r² (here (−0.664)² = 0.441).

6. Comparing coefficients

In a model with both predictors, the coefficient for inequality is −7.15 and for homicides (per million) +0.17. Which conclusion is correct?

  1. Inequality is 40 times more important than homicides
  2. The coefficients are in different units, so their sizes can’t be compared directly; standardized coefficients or R² are needed
  3. Homicides increase trust
  4. Both predictors are equally important
TipAnswer

b. One unit of the inequality ratio and one homicide per million people are not comparable units. Standardized coefficients express both in standard deviations. (And c is wrong twice: the homicide coefficient is tiny and uncertain, and “increase” is causal language.)

7. Holding constant

What does “adjusting for homicide rates” mean for the inequality coefficient in a multiple regression?

  1. Homicides were removed from the data
  2. The coefficient describes the association between inequality and trust among countries with the same homicide rate
  3. We have proved that homicides don’t affect trust
  4. We ran an experiment that held homicides fixed
TipAnswer

b. Multiple regression compares like with like statistically. It is not an experiment, and it only adjusts for the variables actually in the model.

8. A categorical predictor

In a regression of trust on world region with Western Europe as the reference category, the intercept is 40.66 and the coefficient for Asia is −13.96. What is the predicted trust for an Asian country?

  1. −13.96%
  2. 13.96%
  3. 26.70%
  4. 54.62%
TipAnswer

c. 40.66 − 13.96 = 26.70%, which is simply the mean of the Asian countries. Each region’s coefficient is its difference from the reference category.

9. A binary predictor

Protestant countries have mean trust of 48.26% and other countries 24.81%. In a regression of trust on Protestantism (coded 1 = Protestant, 0 = not), what is the coefficient?

  1. 24.81
  2. 48.26
  3. 23.45
  4. 0.36
TipAnswer

c. With a single 0/1 predictor, the coefficient is the difference between the two group means (48.26 − 24.81). The intercept (24.81) is the mean of the group coded 0.

10. Checks

Which of these is not a good reason to doubt a regression line?

  1. The scatter plot shows a curved pattern
  2. One extreme case tilts the line
  3. Two predictors are almost perfectly correlated (high VIF)
  4. The R² is below 0.5
TipAnswer

d. A modest R² is normal in social science and does not make the line wrong: it just means other factors matter too. Curvature (Anscombe’s quartet), influential cases and multicollinearity are genuine reasons for caution.
